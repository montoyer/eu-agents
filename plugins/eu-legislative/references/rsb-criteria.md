## What the Board is (verified)

- An **independent body within the Commission** scrutinising the quality of
  **all impact assessments, all fitness checks and selected evaluations** —
  quality assurance so the political level decides on the best available
  evidence.
- **Composition: a chairperson and eight members (nine total)**, appointed
  full-time for a **three-year non-renewable term** (extendable by up to one
  year in exceptional circumstances). The chair and four members come from
  within the Commission services; **four members are recruited externally**.
- Members act in their personal capacity with collective responsibility; the
  Board takes no instructions from the Commission or any other body.
- Opinions are **published on the Commission website** together with the
  related reports. A Secretariat-General team supports the Board.
- At a DG's request, the Board may at its discretion meet services
  **upstream** on planned IAs, fitness checks or selected evaluations.

## What gets scrutinised (verified)

- Every IA report and every fitness check.
- **Selected evaluations**: the Board notifies DGs early each calendar year of
  the list, based on DGs' evaluation planning, management plans, Decide and
  the Commission work programme.
- Fitness checks/evaluations selected for review are submitted **well in
  advance** of any related IA; if submitted in parallel, the Board may
  scrutinise both at the same meeting.

## Opinions and consequences (verified)

| Opinion | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Positive | Proceed |
| Positive with reservations | Proceed, addressing the reservations |
| Negative (IA, first) | Lead DG incorporates the Board's recommendations into a **revised IA report** and discusses the changes with the interservice coordination group |
| Negative (IA, second) | The lead DG **cannot start inter-service consultation without a derogation granted by the Commissioner responsible for 'better regulation'** |
| Negative (evaluation / fitness check) | Does **not** require resubmission and does not prevent publication of the report |

## What the Board checks for (practice)

`[model knowledge — verify against the current RSB annual report]`

Recurring failure points in negative opinions: weak or missing baseline,
options constructed so the preferred one wins by default, unquantified costs
and benefits, consultation results asserted rather than analysed, boilerplate
subsidiarity statements, intervention logic gaps in evaluations.

Submission package for evaluations/fitness checks (verified): note signed by
the Director-General to the RSB Chair, the draft SWD, its executive summary
where applicable, and the minutes of the last interservice coordination group
meeting on the draft.
