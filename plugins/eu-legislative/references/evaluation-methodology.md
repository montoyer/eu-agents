## When required (Tool #45)

- **Evaluation**: ex-post assessment of a single intervention.
- **Fitness check**: comprehensive evaluation of **several related
  interventions** in a policy area, applying the same five criteria from a
  sectoral perspective, looking for synergies, overlaps, gaps and cumulative
  burden.
- **"Evaluate first"**: a revision proposal should be preceded by an
  evaluation of the existing act; the evaluation feeds the IA's baseline.

## The five evaluation criteria (Tool #47, verified)

**Effectiveness · efficiency · coherence · relevance · EU added value.**

| Criterion | Core question |
|---|---|
| Effectiveness | Were the objectives achieved? What explains observed change (attribution vs contribution)? |
| Efficiency | Are costs proportionate to benefits? Simplification and burden-reduction potential quantified? |
| Coherence | Internally consistent, and consistent with other EU interventions and obligations? |
| Relevance | Do objectives still match current needs? |
| EU added value | What would be lost without the EU intervention (counterfactual)? |

## Designing the evaluation (Tool #46 — six verified design steps)

1. **Clarify the purpose** — why the evaluation is carried out, what findings
   it should provide, how they will be used; stated in the call for evidence.
2. **Define the scope** — what exactly is evaluated (instruments, period,
   geography).
3. **Explain the intervention logic** — how the intervention was expected to
   work; draw on the original IA or explanatory memorandum.
4. **Draft good evaluation questions** — addressing the five criteria and
   issues of known stakeholder interest, considering call-for-evidence
   feedback.
5. **Identify the point(s) of comparison** — benchmarks, projections from the
   original IA's preferred option, or measurements at the intervention's
   start; update them for co-legislators' changes to the proposal.
6. **Choose data collection and analytical methods** — fill data gaps and
   answer the questions robustly (methods: Tools #67–#68, Chapter 8).

## Conducting (Tool #48 — verified sequence)

(1) **Call for evidence** → (2) **interservice coordination group** →
(3) conduct of the evaluation → (4) **stakeholder consultation** (12-week
public consultation per Chapter 7 standards — see
`references/consultation-standards.md`).

## Report and scrutiny (Tools #49, #3)

The evaluation report is an SWD; fitness checks and **selected evaluations**
are scrutinised by the RSB (list notified to DGs early each calendar year). A
negative RSB opinion on an evaluation does **not** require resubmission — see
`references/rsb-criteria.md`.

## Back-to-back evaluation and IA (Tool #50, verified)

Normally sequential (evaluation results feed the IA). Where timing forces it,
evaluation and IA may be launched simultaneously and run as a **single
process** — the intention must be announced (call for evidence) and the
evaluation findings must still be identifiable in the IA's problem definition
and baseline `[model knowledge — verify procedural details against Tool #50]`.
