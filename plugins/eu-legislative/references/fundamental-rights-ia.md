## Obligation (verified)

- **All Commission acts and initiatives must comply with the Charter**; EU
  legal acts can be challenged before the CJEU for non-compliance.
- Tool #29 ("Fundamental rights, including the promotion of equality") guides
  the identification and assessment of fundamental-rights impacts in IAs, and
  applies equally in **evaluations** — especially where the original IA had to
  address fundamental-rights issues.
- The Charter's six substantive titles (Dignity, Freedoms, Equality,
  Solidarity, Citizens' Rights, Justice) plus the General provisions title
  apply to **all EU policies** and all institutions, bodies and agencies.

## The two classes of rights (verified)

- **Absolute rights** — cannot be limited or restricted **no matter how
  important the policy objective** (e.g. human dignity, prohibition of
  torture `[model knowledge — verify Box 2 list]`).
- **Limitable rights** — restrictions possible only under the **strict
  requirements of Article 52 of the Charter**: provided for by law, respecting
  the essence, genuinely meeting a general-interest objective, and
  proportionate — apply the four-step test in `references/eu-charter.md`.

## Step-by-step assessment (verified structure)

1. **Problem definition** — fundamental rights may be the very reason for
   acting (protecting individuals against interferences in the policy area).
2. **Screening** — identify the Charter articles engaged by each option (use
   the rights-engagement table in `references/eu-charter.md`).
3. **Assessment** — for each engaged right: is it absolute or limitable; for
   limitations, run the Art. 52(1) analysis per option, including impacts on
   **equality and non-discrimination** (the tool's explicit equality focus).
4. **Reporting** — fundamental-rights impacts are among the issues the IA
   report must systematically address (Tool #11); name the engaged articles in
   the proposal's fundamental-rights recital.
