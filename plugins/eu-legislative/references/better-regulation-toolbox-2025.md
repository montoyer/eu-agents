## When which instrument is required

| Initiative | Required |
|---|---|
| Legislative proposal / significant impacts expected | **Impact assessment (IA)** + RSB positive opinion |
| Significant impacts but IA impossible (urgency) | Staff Working Document within 3 months / analytical document + justification |
| Evaluation of existing act | **Evaluation** (single act) or **fitness check** (several related acts), applying the 5 criteria |
| Any IA or evaluation | **Public consultation** (12 weeks, all official languages for major initiatives) and a published Call for Evidence |
| Revision proposals | **"Evaluate first"** principle — no revision IA without a prior evaluation |

## The IA core analytical chain

1. **Problem definition** — what is the problem, scale (baseline data), drivers,
   who is affected, why does it persist (market failure, regulatory failure,
   behavioural bias)? The problem must be evidenced, not asserted.
2. **Why should the EU act** — legal basis + the subsidiarity grid (**Tool #5** ✓):
   the grid recommended by the Task Force on Subsidiarity is annexed **as a
   staff working document to significant or politically sensitive
   initiatives** ✓ — see `references/subsidiarity-protocol.md`.
3. **Objectives** — general → specific, SMART, linked to the problem drivers.
4. **Policy options** — always include the **baseline (no-EU-action) option**;
   discarded options must be listed with reasons; consider non-regulatory and
   minimum-harmonisation variants (**Tool #17** Choice of policy instruments ✓).
   Scale the depth of analysis to the initiative (**Tool #12** Proportionality
   of the IA ✓).
5. **Impacts** — economic, social, environmental; screen for: SMEs (SME test),
   competitiveness, fundamental rights, consumers, digital ("digital by
   default"), climate/environment (DNSH — do no significant harm,
   consistency with climate neutrality), territorial dimension.
6. **Comparison of options** — effectiveness, efficiency, coherence,
   proportionality; preferred option justified against the baseline.
7. **Monitoring and evaluation arrangements** — indicators, data sources,
   review clause.

## Economic methods (Chapter 8 — Methodologies)

Cost/benefit typology: **Tool #56** ✓; CBA: **Tool #63** ✓.

| Method | Use when | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost-benefit analysis (CBA)** (Tool #63 ✓) | Impacts monetisable on both sides | Social discount factor: **3 % in real terms** ✓ (Tool #64; lowered from 4 % in the 2017 edition; test lower/higher values in sensitivity analysis) ✓; report NPV — both NPV and BCR are highly sensitive to the rate chosen ✓ |
| **Cost-effectiveness analysis (CEA)** | Benefits hard to monetise but quantifiable (e.g. lives saved, tCO₂) | Cost per unit of outcome across options |
| **Multi-criteria analysis (MCA)** | Mixed quantitative/qualitative evidence | Explicit weighting, transparent scoring matrix |
| **Standard Cost Model** | Administrative burden | Price × quantity (tariff × time × frequency × population) |
| Modelling (CGE, partial equilibrium, micro-simulation) | Economy-wide or sectoral effects | JRC models (GEM-E3, QUEST) — document assumptions `[model knowledge — verify]` |

**Cost typology** (Tool #56 ✓): direct compliance costs (charges, substantive
compliance, administrative burden), enforcement costs, indirect costs;
benefits mirrored.
**One In, One Out (OIOO)** (Tool #59 — Cost estimates and the 'one in, one
out' approach ✓): new administrative burdens on businesses must be offset by
equivalent reductions in the same policy area — quantify and state the offset
in the IA.

## The SME test (Tool #23 ✓, 4 steps) ✓

1. **Identification of affected businesses and assessment of relevance** ✓ —
   threshold question: *"To what extent is the initiative relevant for SMEs?"*
   ('not relevant' / 'relevant' / 'highly relevant') ✓. If 'not relevant', the
   SME test may stop at step 1 ✓.
2. **Consultation of SME stakeholders** ✓.
3. **Assessment of the impact on SMEs** ✓ — for 'highly relevant' initiatives
   an in-depth analysis **by size-class** is required ✓; indirect/trickle-down
   effects must not be underestimated even where SMEs are exempted ✓.
4. **Minimising negative impacts on SMEs** ✓ — exemptions for
   micro-enterprises, lighter regimes, transition periods
   `[model knowledge — verify mitigation menu]`.

Process anchors ✓: since 2022 the **Network of SME Envoys** flags initiatives
through the **'SME filter'**; since **May 2025** IAs for 'relevant' or 'highly
relevant' initiatives must include a mandatory **'SME Check' annex** reporting
the test results.

## Consultation standards (Tools #51–#52 ✓)

- Consultation strategy (Tool #52 ✓) defined at IA inception: target groups mapped
  (stakeholder mapping), methods (open public consultation, targeted surveys,
  workshops, SME panels), languages.
- **Open public consultation: minimum 12 weeks**, on the Have Your Say portal,
  for initiatives with IAs; questionnaire structured so non-specialists can
  answer; closed questions + free text.
- Question design: neutral wording, no leading questions, every IA option
  testable from the responses; publish a synopsis report covering **all**
  consultation activities and explain how input was used (feedback duty).

## Evaluation and fitness checks — the 5 criteria (Tools #45, #47 ✓)

Criteria verified ✓: **effectiveness, efficiency, coherence, relevance, and EU
added value**.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| **Effectiveness** | Have the objectives been achieved? What caused observed change (attribution vs contribution)? |
| **Efficiency** | Are costs proportionate to benefits? Simplification and burden-reduction potential (REFIT) quantified? |
| **Relevance** | Do the objectives still match current needs? |
| **Coherence** | Internal coherence and coherence with other EU interventions and international obligations |
| **EU added value** | What would be lost if the intervention were withdrawn (counterfactual)? |

Evaluations follow the same evidence standards as IAs (intervention logic,
baseline reconstruction, stakeholder consultation) and feed the "evaluate
first" principle.

## RSB scrutiny — what the Board checks (Tool #3 ✓)

The Regulatory Scrutiny Board issues **positive / positive with reservations /
negative** opinions ✓.

- **Impact assessments**: after a negative opinion the lead DG incorporates
  the Board's recommendations into a revised IA report and discusses the
  changes with the interservice coordination group ✓. After a **second
  negative opinion, the lead DG cannot start the inter-service consultation
  without a derogation granted by the Commissioner responsible for 'better
  regulation'** ✓.
- **Evaluations and fitness checks** selected for scrutiny: a negative opinion
  does **not** require resubmission ✓.

Recurring failure points: weak baseline, options designed to make the
preferred one win, unquantified costs, consultation results asserted rather
than analysed, subsidiarity boilerplate
`[model knowledge — verify against current RSB annual report]`.
