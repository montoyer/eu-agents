## The core doctrine

**The choice of legal basis must rest on objective factors amenable to
judicial review — in particular the aim and content of the measure.**
(C-45/86 *Commission v Council (Generalised Tariff Preferences)*; settled
case law) `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]`

A wrong legal basis is not a formality: it determines procedure (EP role,
voting rule) and competence, and is a ground for **annulment under Art. 263
TFEU** as infringement of the Treaties — and may constitute infringement of an
essential procedural requirement where the EP's prerogatives are affected.

## Centre of gravity (predominant purpose) test

| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| C-300/89 *Commission v Council (Titanium Dioxide)* | Where a measure pursues two aims, the **main or predominant** one determines the single legal basis. Dual basis is excluded where the procedures attached are **incompatible** (then: cooperation with EP vs unanimity consultation) `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |
| Opinion 2/00 *(Cartagena Protocol)* | Exceptionally, a measure pursuing **several inseparable objectives, none secondary**, may rest on the corresponding multiple bases — only if procedures are compatible `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |
| C-338/01 *Commission v Council* | Combining bases is impermissible where one requires unanimity and the other QMV — procedure incompatibility `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |
| C-411/06 *Commission v Parliament and Council (Waste shipments)* | Confirms aim-and-content analysis for environment (Art. 192) vs commercial policy (Art. 207) boundary `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |

**Drafting consequence**: state the predominant aim in the recitals and keep
content aligned with it; ancillary effects on other policies do not require
(or permit) an additional basis.

## Internal market — Art. 114 TFEU limits

| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| C-376/98 *Germany v Parliament and Council (Tobacco Advertising I)* | Art. 114 is **not a general power to regulate the internal market**: the measure must genuinely have as its object the improvement of the conditions for the establishment and functioning of the internal market — removing obstacles to trade or appreciable distortions of competition. First annulment of an entire act for wrong basis `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |
| C-380/03 *Tobacco Advertising II* | Recourse to Art. 114 is possible to prevent **likely future obstacles** from diverging national laws — emergence must be likely, not speculative `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |
| C-58/08 *Vodafone (Roaming)* | Art. 114 supports intervention (price caps) where divergent national approaches threaten the internal market — broad confirmation post-*Tobacco* `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |
| C-270/12 *UK v Parliament and Council (ESMA short-selling)* | Art. 114 can support conferring intervention powers on an EU agency `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |

## Boundary disputes worth knowing

| Boundary | Case | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| CFSP vs AFSJ (sanctions) | C-130/10 *Parliament v Council (Al-Qaida smart sanctions)* | Art. 215 TFEU (CFSP-linked), not Art. 75; EP role differs `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |
| CFSP vs development cooperation | C-263/14 *Parliament v Council (Tanzania transfer agreement)* | Predominant CFSP purpose — procedure under Art. 218 follows the substantive basis `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |
| Commercial policy vs transport | Opinion 2/15 *(Singapore FTA)* | Scope of exclusive CCP competence vs shared elements (portfolio investment, ISDS) `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |
| Art. 352 limits | Opinion 2/94 *(ECHR accession I)* | The flexibility clause cannot be used to widen Union competences beyond the Treaty framework `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |
| Essential elements reserved to legislator | C-355/10 *Parliament v Council (Frontex border surveillance)* | Politically sensitive choices = essential elements; cannot be left to implementing measures `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |
| Delegated vs implementing | C-427/12 *Commission v Parliament and Council (Biocides)* | Legislator has discretion; review limited to manifest error — does the act **supplement/amend** (Art. 290) or **implement uniformly** (Art. 291)? `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]` |

## Checklist for the legal-basis section of a proposal

1. Identify aim (recitals) and content (operative articles) — objectively.
2. Single predominant purpose → single basis; genuinely inseparable dual
   purpose → dual basis **only if procedures are compatible**.
3. If Art. 114: document the obstacle/distortion (existing or likely) with
   evidence — assertion is not enough.
4. Check the basis's procedural consequences (OLP vs special; unanimity;
   EP consent/consultation) and any Protocol No 2 variant (Art. 76 TFEU).
5. Reserve essential elements to the act itself; map Art. 290/291 choices.
