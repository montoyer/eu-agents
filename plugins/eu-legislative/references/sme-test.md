## SME definition (Recommendation 2003/361/EC)

| Class | Staff (obligatory) | And either turnover | Or balance sheet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medium | < 250 | < EUR 50 m | < EUR 43 m |
| Small | < 50 | ≤ EUR 10 m | ≤ EUR 10 m |
| Micro | < 10 | ≤ EUR 2 m | ≤ EUR 2 m |

The headcount threshold is obligatory (> 250 staff can never be an SME);
turnover/balance-sheet may be chosen at the firm's advantage. Thresholds may be
exceeded for one year without losing status; two consecutive years loses it.
SMEs are > 99 % of EU-27 non-financial businesses; > 90 % are micro.

## Process anchors

- **'Think small first'** is the anchoring principle of SME policymaking.
- Since 2022, the **Network of SME Envoys** identifies, via the **'SME
  filter'**, initiatives likely to significantly affect SMEs.
- Since **May 2025**, IAs for initiatives 'relevant' or 'highly relevant' for
  SMEs must include a mandatory **'SME Check' annex** reporting the test
  results.

## The four steps

### Step 1 — Identification of affected businesses and assessment of relevance

Threshold question: *"To what extent is the initiative relevant for SMEs?"* —
**'not relevant' / 'relevant' / 'highly relevant'**. Guiding questions:
- Are SMEs within the scope of the initiative? Does it specifically target them?
- Will SMEs be significantly impacted, directly or indirectly?
- Are impacts on SMEs likely to be more substantial than on other companies?

'Not relevant' → the SME test may stop at step 1 (stated in the IA report).
'Relevant' / 'highly relevant' → steps 2–4 apply and feed the SME Check annex.
Even where SMEs are exempted from scope, **indirect trickle-down effects must
not be underestimated** — SMEs rarely have leverage to counter them.

### Step 2 — Consultation of SME stakeholders

- What are the consultation strategy's tools to reach the SME community?
- Is a targeted consultation envisaged (SME panel, workshop, reality checks)?
- Eurostat **Structural Business Statistics** are the starting point for
  identifying the affected population.

### Step 3 — Assessment of the impact on SMEs

- For **'highly relevant'** initiatives: in-depth analysis **by size-class**
  (micro / small / medium).
- Quantify compliance costs using Tools #56–#57 (cost typology and methods);
  results also feed the competitiveness check annex (Tool #21).
- SMEs bear disproportionate costs: fewer economies of scale, less capacity to
  absorb fixed costs, costlier capital, weaker bargaining power.

### Step 4 — Minimising negative impacts on SMEs

Guiding questions: are specific SME subgroups more exposed; is an SME-friendly
option available; if not, which mitigating measures go into the preferred
option; which SME-friendly provisions are in the final proposal and why?

**A well-designed proposal is SME-friendly by default** — mitigation is the
fallback, not the plan. Verified mitigation menu:
- simplified reporting (synergies with existing obligations, lower frequency,
  simplified templates) and simplified obligations;
- support measures: information and training campaigns, guidelines, IT tools,
  one-stop-shops / helpdesks / hubs;
- digital compliance assistance tools; lighter treatment for third-party
  certified companies; common translated forms;
- reduced fees, additional financial support, proportionate
  sanctions/administrative fines;
- self-certification; longer validity of administrative certificates.

Member States are encouraged to run a national SME test when transposing,
since gold-plating often adds the burden.
