## General principles (Guidelines 1–6)

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | Drafting shall be **clear** (easily understood, unambiguous), **simple** (concise, free of superfluous elements) and **precise** (leaving no doubt in the reader's mind) |
| 2 | Drafting shall be appropriate to the **type of act** — a regulation is directly applicable and must not be re-stated in national law; a directive binds as to result and must leave transposition choices to Member States; never draft a "false directive" so detailed it leaves nothing to transpose |
| 3 | Drafting shall take account of the **persons to whom the act applies** — specialists vs the general public — and aim for enforceability |
| 4 | Provisions shall be **concise**; avoid overly long articles and sentences, one idea per sentence |
| 5 | Drafting shall use terms that work in **all official languages** — no idioms, no language-specific legal concepts without definition |
| 6 | **Terminology shall be consistent** — internally within the act, and externally with the acquis in the same field; the same term for the same concept, never synonyms for elegant variation |

## Structure of an act (Guidelines 7–15)

Standard order — **title → preamble (citations + recitals) → enacting terms →
annexes**:

- **Title** (G.8): succinct, full indication of subject matter; misleading or
  over-broad titles rejected by the legal revisers.
- **Citations** (G.9): the legal basis (`Having regard to … Article X TFEU`),
  procedural references, and consultations — in that order. The legal basis
  article must be cited precisely (paragraph, subparagraph).
- **Recitals** (G.10–11): state the reasons in concise, **non-normative**
  terms, numbered (1), (2), …. A recital justifies an article; it must not
  repeat it verbatim, contain normative obligations, or contradict the
  enacting terms. Each substantive choice (legal basis, subsidiarity,
  proportionality, delegations) needs a recital.
- **Enacting terms** (G.12–15): articles are the basic unit; grouped where
  needed into Parts → Titles → Chapters → Sections. Subdivision of an article:
  numbered paragraphs (1., 2.), subparagraphs, points ((a), (b); (i), (ii)).
  Standard article order: subject matter and scope → definitions →
  substantive obligations → procedural/institutional provisions → delegated
  and implementing powers → penalties → transitional and final provisions.
- **Non-normative content is banned** from enacting terms (G.12): no wishes,
  declarations, or statements of fact.

## Definitions (Guideline 14)

- Define a term only if it departs from ordinary or established legal meaning.
- Definitions must contain **no normative content** (no hidden obligations).
- A defined term must be used **uniformly** throughout, and the definition must
  align with the acquis (do not redefine GDPR, CRR, etc. terms without reason).

## References, amendments, repeals (Guidelines 16–21)

- **References** (G.16): keep internal and external references to a minimum;
  never circular or serial (a reference to a reference). Distinguish **static**
  references (to an act "as amended by …", frozen) from **dynamic** references
  (evolving with the target act) — dynamic is the default within EU law but
  must be deliberate.
- **Amendments** (G.18): amend by **textual replacement** ("Article X is
  replaced by the following: …") — no disguised substantive amendment inside a
  recast or a non-amending act; do not amend an amending act.
- **Dates and deadlines** (G.20): express absolutely ("by 31 December 2027"),
  never "within two years of entry into force" unless intentional.
- **Repeals** (G.21): repeal explicitly and list repealed acts; include a
  correlation table when recasting.
- **Entry into force / application** (G.20): default is the twentieth day
  following OJ publication; distinguish entry into force from date of
  application (deferred application is the norm for regulations needing
  preparation).

## Annexes (Guideline 22)

Technical content only (lists, tables, forms, methodologies); each annex must
be **referred to in the enacting terms**, and carries the same legal force.

## Delegated and implementing acts — drafting standard

`[model knowledge — verify against the 2016 IIA and its Common Understanding]`

- **Art. 290 TFEU (delegated)**: empowerment must state **objectives, content,
  scope and duration**; essential elements are reserved to the legislative act
  and cannot be delegated. Standard clauses: duration (indeterminate or X
  years, tacitly extended), revocation, 2-month objection period (+2 months
  extension), Member State expert consultation before adoption.
- **Art. 291 TFEU (implementing)**: uniform conditions for implementation;
  comitology procedures per Regulation (EU) No 182/2011 — see
  `references/comitology-reg-182-2011.md`.
- Choice test: does the act **amend or supplement** the basic act (→ delegated)
  or merely **implement it uniformly** (→ implementing)? (C-427/12 *Biocides*)
  `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]`.

## Legal-linguistic revision (trilogue stage)

Before signature, jurist-linguists of EP and Council finalise the text in all
official languages. For the four-column document: only the agreed compromise
column is revised; drafting defects spotted at revision cannot reopen the
political agreement — flag JPG breaches **before** the final trilogue.
