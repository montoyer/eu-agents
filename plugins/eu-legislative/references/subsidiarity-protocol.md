## The two principles (Art. 5 TEU)

| Principle | Test | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Subsidiarity** (Art. 5(3) TEU) | In areas of non-exclusive competence, the EU acts only if and in so far as the objectives **cannot be sufficiently achieved by the Member States** (centrally, regionally or locally) **and can, by reason of scale or effects, be better achieved at Union level** | Shared and supporting competences only — never exclusive competence (Art. 3 TFEU) |
| **Proportionality** (Art. 5(4) TEU) | The content and form of Union action **shall not exceed what is necessary** to achieve the objectives of the Treaties | All Union action, including exclusive competence |

The subsidiarity test is therefore **two-limbed**: (1) national insufficiency
and (2) Union added value. Both limbs must be argued with qualitative and,
wherever possible, quantitative indicators (Art. 5 of the Protocol).

## Article-by-article summary

| Art. | Obligation |
|---|---|
| 1 | Each institution shall ensure constant respect for subsidiarity and proportionality |
| 2 | The Commission shall **consult widely** before proposing legislative acts, taking the regional and local dimension into account; urgency exceptions must be reasoned |
| 3 | "Draft legislative act" covers Commission proposals, Parliament/Council initiatives, ECB/CJEU/EIB requests and Member State initiatives |
| 4 | All draft legislative acts (and amended drafts, and legislative resolutions/positions) are forwarded to **national parliaments** at the same time as to the Union legislator |
| 5 | Every draft must contain a **detailed statement** allowing appraisal of subsidiarity and proportionality compliance: qualitative and where possible quantitative substantiation, assessment of financial impact and (for directives) of implementation burden, and reasons why the objective is better achieved at Union level. Burdens must be **minimised and commensurate** |
| 6 | Any national parliament (or chamber) may, **within 8 weeks** of transmission, send a **reasoned opinion** stating why the draft does not comply with subsidiarity |
| 7 | Vote thresholds and the yellow/orange card mechanisms (below) |
| 8 | The **CJEU has jurisdiction** over subsidiarity-infringement actions brought under Art. 263 TFEU by Member States (including on behalf of their national parliament) and by the **Committee of the Regions** for acts on which it must be consulted |
| 9 | The Commission submits an **annual report** on the application of Art. 5 TEU to the European Council, EP, Council and national parliaments |

## The early warning mechanism (Arts. 6–7)

**Vote allocation**: each national parliament has **2 votes** (one per chamber
in bicameral systems) — 27 Member States × 2 = **54 votes total**.

| Mechanism | Threshold | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Reasoned opinion** | any single parliament/chamber, within 8 weeks | The institutions "shall take account" of it |
| **Yellow card** (Art. 7(2)) | ≥ **1/3** of all votes (**18/54**); lowered to ≥ **1/4** (**14/54**) for drafts under Art. 76 TFEU (judicial cooperation in criminal matters, police cooperation) | The draft **must be reviewed**; the initiator may maintain, amend or withdraw, **giving reasons** |
| **Orange card** (Art. 7(3), OLP only) | **simple majority** of votes (**28/54**) | Commission reviews; if it maintains the proposal it must justify in a reasoned opinion to the legislator. Before concluding first reading, the proposal falls if **55 % of Council members** or a **majority of votes cast in the EP** find it incompatible with subsidiarity |

Yellow cards raised to date: Monti II (2012 — withdrawn), EPPO (2013 —
maintained), Posted Workers revision (2016 — maintained)

## Drafting the subsidiarity statement (practical)

Every legislative proposal's explanatory memorandum and impact assessment must
answer, with evidence, the **subsidiarity grid** questions (BR Toolbox Dec
2025, Tool #5 — the grid is annexed as a staff working document to significant
or politically sensitive initiatives):

1. Does the Union have competence, and what is the legal basis?
2. Why can the objectives not be sufficiently achieved by Member States alone
   (necessity — cross-border dimension, internal-market fragmentation,
   collective-action failure)?
3. What is the Union added value (scale, effects, legal certainty)?
4. Is the instrument and intensity proportionate (directive vs regulation,
   minimum vs full harmonisation, sunset/review clauses)?

CJEU review is light-touch — manifest error standard (C-84/94 *UK v Council*,
C-491/01 *BAT*) — but a missing or boilerplate statement is an essential
procedural requirement risk `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]`.
