## Two-step method (verified figures)

**Step 1 — basic amount:**
- up to **30 % of the value of sales** of goods/services to which the
  infringement relates, in the relevant geographic area within the EEA
  (normally the last full business year of participation);
- multiplied by the **number of years** of participation (periods < 6 months
  = half year; > 6 months = full year);
- plus the **"entry fee"**: between **15 % and 25 %** of the value of sales
  added irrespective of duration, for cartels (price fixing, market sharing,
  output limitation) — and possible for other infringements.

**Step 2 — adjustments:**
- **Aggravating** circumstances: recidivism (up to **100 % increase per prior
  finding**), refusal to cooperate/obstruction, leader or instigator role.
- **Mitigating**: substantially limited involvement, termination on first
  intervention (not for cartels), negligence, cooperation outside leniency,
  conduct authorised/encouraged by public authorities.
- **Deterrence**: possible increase for undertakings with particularly large
  turnover beyond the relevant sales; gravity reflects nature, combined
  market share, geographic scope, implementation.

## Caps and reductions (verified)

- **Legal maximum: 10 % of total turnover** in the preceding business year
  (Art. 23(2) Reg. 1/2003).
- **Leniency** reductions apply per the 2006 Leniency Notice (immunity or
  bands of reduction) — separate instrument
  `[EUR-Lex — verify Leniency Notice 2006/C 298/11]`.
- **Inability to pay** (point 35): exceptional reduction on evidence that the
  fine would irretrievably jeopardise economic viability.
- **Point 37**: the Commission may depart from the methodology where the
  particularities of the case or deterrence so justify (used in settlement
  and hybrid cases).

## Practice notes

Settlement procedure adds a fixed **10 % settlement reduction** (Settlement
Notice 2008, separate instrument) `[EUR-Lex — verify Settlement Notice
2008/C 167/01]`. Fines are imposed on the undertaking (parental liability per
*Akzo* presumption for wholly-owned subsidiaries)
`[CJEU — verify Curia reference]`.
