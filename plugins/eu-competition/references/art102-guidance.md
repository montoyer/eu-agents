## Dominance (verified)

- Dominance = position of economic strength enabling behaviour to an
  appreciable extent **independently** of competitors, customers and
  consumers (*United Brands* formulation) `[CJEU — verify Curia reference]`.
- Market-share screen (verified): dominance is **not likely below 40 %** in
  the relevant market — but possible in specific cases below that level;
  shares are a first indication, weighed with expansion/entry constraints and
  countervailing buyer power.

## The central concept: anti-competitive foreclosure (verified)

The Commission prioritises conduct leading to **anti-competitive
foreclosure**: access of actual or potential rivals to supplies or markets is
hampered or eliminated, **to the detriment of consumers** (higher prices,
restricted quality/choice/innovation). Factors: position of the dominant
undertaking, market conditions, position of rivals and customers, extent and
duration of the conduct, evidence of actual foreclosure, direct exclusionary
strategy evidence.

## Price-based conduct — the as-efficient-competitor benchmark (verified)

Price-based exclusion is generally pursued only where it could foreclose a
competitor **as efficient** as the dominant firm. Cost benchmarks:
- **AAC** (average avoidable cost) — pricing below AAC indicates sacrifice;
- **LRAIC** (long-run average incremental cost) — pricing below LRAIC may
  foreclose an as-efficient competitor.

## Specific forms (verified structure)

| Conduct | Analytical focus |
|---|---|
| Exclusive dealing / exclusivity rebates | Foreclosure of contestable share of demand |
| Conditional (retroactive) rebates | Effective price over the contestable portion vs AAC/LRAIC |
| Tying and bundling | Distinct products, foreclosure on the tied/bundled market; multi-product rebates |
| Predation | Sacrifice (below-AAC pricing or loss-making strategy) + likely foreclosure; recoupment not a separate precondition under EU law |
| Refusal to supply / margin squeeze | Indispensability of the input, elimination of effective competition downstream, consumer harm; margin squeeze: spread insufficient for an as-efficient downstream rival |

## Defences (verified)

Objective necessity, and **efficiencies** — four cumulative conditions:
efficiencies realised because of the conduct; no less anti-competitive
alternative; efficiencies outweigh competitive harm; no elimination of
effective competition.
