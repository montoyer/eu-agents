## Purpose and core concepts

- Market definition identifies the **competitive constraints** facing the
  undertakings: **demand substitution** (the immediate and most effective
  constraint), **supply substitution** (where suppliers can switch production
  in the short term without significant additional costs or risks), and
  potential competition (considered at the competitive-assessment stage, not
  market definition).
- Two dimensions: **relevant product market** (products/services regarded as
  interchangeable by reason of characteristics, prices and intended use) and
  **relevant geographic market** (area with sufficiently homogeneous
  conditions of competition, distinguishable from neighbouring areas).

## The SSNIP framework

The hypothetical monopolist test: would a **small but significant
non-transitory increase in price** (typically 5–10 %) be profitable for a
hypothetical monopolist of the candidate market? If customers would switch
enough to make it unprofitable, the market is wider. Caveats (verified
themes of the 2024 Notice):
- the **cellophane fallacy** — prevailing prices may already reflect market
  power, especially in Art. 102 cases;
- SSNIP is a **conceptual framework**, not a mandatory empirical test; the
  Commission uses all available evidence.

## 2024 modernisation themes (verified)

- **Digital markets**: multi-sided platforms (define markets for the products
  offered by the platform as a whole or separately per side), ecosystems,
  after-markets and bundles.
- **Zero-price products**: where a price rise is not meaningful, assess a
  small but significant **non-transitory decrease in quality (SSNDQ)** or
  other non-price parameters (attention, data, privacy).
- **Innovation-intensive industries**: account for pipeline products and
  innovation competition even before products reach the market.
- **Forward-looking assessment**: structural market changes (digitalisation,
  sustainability transitions) may justify departing from past patterns.
- **Geographic dimension**: imports, global competition and the role of trade
  flows; markets can be national, EEA-wide or worldwide — defined by
  conditions of competition, not by the undertaking's footprint.

## Market shares (verified themes)

Sales values/volumes are the usual basis, but the Notice endorses alternative
metrics where they better reflect competitive position: **capacity, number of
users, frequency of use, downloads, engagement metrics** for digital services,
and pipeline significance in innovation markets.
