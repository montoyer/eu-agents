## Priority categories (verified — "as a matter of priority")

1. **Non-communication** of transposition measures, or measures that
   **incorrectly transposed** directives;
2. Failure to comply with a Court judgment (**Art. 260(2) TFEU**);
3. **Serious damage to EU financial interests**;
4. Violation of **EU exclusive powers** (Art. 2(1) read with Art. 3 TFEU).

## High priority: systemic weaknesses (verified)

Infringements revealing **systemic weaknesses that undermine the EU's
institutional framework**, in particular those affecting **national judicial
systems**. The Commission pursues rigorously:
- national rules or general practices **impeding the preliminary-ruling
  procedure** (Art. 267 TFEU);
- national law preventing courts from acknowledging the **primacy of EU
  law**;
- absence of **effective redress** for breaches of EU law (Art. 47 Charter,
  rule of law).

## Also weighted (verified)

- Breaches of the **four fundamental freedoms** creating particular problems
  for citizens/businesses operating cross-border, or with **systemic impact
  beyond one Member State**;
- national legislation that fails to comply with EU law (systematically
  undermines citizens' rights);
- **persistent failure** by a Member State to apply EU law correctly.

## Where the Commission exercises discretion *not* to pursue (verified)

- **Preliminary-ruling proceedings pending** on the same issue, where
  Commission action would not significantly accelerate resolution;
- cases contradicting the line taken by the College in a **legislative
  proposal**;
- **individual cases of incorrect application** without wider principle —
  complainants directed to national remedies where effective protection
  exists;
- cases closed "when it considers this to be appropriate from a policy point
  of view", judged by the **added value** of the procedure.

## Drafting consequence

An LFN or referral recommendation should state explicitly **which priority
category the case falls into** and, where discretion factors point against
pursuit, address them — this mirrors the Commission's own published
decision-frame.
