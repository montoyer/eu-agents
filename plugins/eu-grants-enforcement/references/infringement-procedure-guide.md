## The Commission's posture (verified)

- Guardian of the Treaties (Art. 17(1) TEU); **discretionary power** whether
  and when to open an infringement procedure or refer to the Court — settled
  case law: individuals cannot compel the Commission to pursue a case.
- Strategy: "bigger and more ambitious on big things, smaller and more modest
  on small things" — enforcement focused on the most important breaches; see
  the priority categories in `references/infringement-priorities.md`.

## EU Pilot scaled back (verified)

EU Pilot was set up to resolve potential breaches early — but under this
Communication the Commission launches infringement procedures **without
relying on the EU Pilot mechanism, unless recourse to EU Pilot is seen as
useful in a given case**. Treat EU Pilot as the exception, not a mandatory
pre-stage — see `references/eu-pilot-guide.md`.

## Art. 260(3) TFEU — non-communication cases (verified policy change)

For failure to **transpose a legislative directive**:

- Old practice (2011 Communication): usually request **only a periodic
  penalty payment**.
- **Adjusted practice (verified)**: the Commission **systematically asks the
  Court to impose a lump sum as well as a periodic penalty payment**; the
  extent of (partial) transposition feeds the seriousness factor of the lump
  sum.
- Consequence (verified): where the Member State transposes **during the
  court proceedings**, the Commission **no longer withdraws the action** —
  the penalty payment loses its purpose, but the **lump sum** (punishing the
  duration of the infringement) remains; the Commission informs the Court
  without delay when an infringement ends.
- Transitional rule (verified): applies to procedures whose **LFN decision**
  post-dates the Communication's publication.
- The Commission distinguishes carefully between **incorrect transposition**
  and **(partial) lack of transposition** — only the latter is the
  Art. 260(3) route.

## Complaint handling (verified themes)

Complainants remain an essential detection channel, but:
- individual cases of incorrect application **without wider principle**
  (no general practice, no conformity problem, no systemic failure) are, as a
  general rule, **directed to national redress** where effective legal
  protection exists;
- complaints raising transposition shortcomings in a general way are folded
  into the **compliance assessment** of the directive rather than pursued
  individually;
- standard administrative benchmarks (12-month investigation benchmark and
  2-month Member State reply deadlines for LFN/RO) continue to apply
  `[model knowledge — verify against the 2002/2012 complaint-handling
  communications]`.

## Compliance assessment (verified)

A more structured, systematic assessment of **transposition and conformity**
of national measures, supported by data-analytics tooling for Single Market
legislation — speeding up gap detection, incorrect transposition and possible
**gold-plating** identification. See `references/transposition-guide.md`.
