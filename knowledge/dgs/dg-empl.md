# DG EMPL — Directorate-General for Employment, Social Affairs and Inclusion

> Supports the Commissioner for Jobs and Social Rights (or equivalent portfolio).

---

## Institutional role

DG EMPL is responsible for EU employment and social policy. It manages the EU's largest structural investment fund — the European Social Fund Plus (ESF+) — and is the legislative home of EU labour law, social security coordination, and the European Pillar of Social Rights. DG EMPL bridges regulatory work (minimum wages, working conditions, social security portability) with fund management (ESF+ disbursed through member states).

DG EMPL's policy remit includes a tension built into the treaties: social policy is a shared competence, but most labour regulation requires unanimous Council votes for sensitive areas (pay, freedom of association, industrial action), leaving DG EMPL relying on qualified majority voting only for working conditions, anti-discrimination, and social security coordination.

## Legal basis

- **Social policy (shared competence):** TFEU Art. 151–161 (Title X — Social Policy)
- **Art. 153(1):** QMV applies to: working conditions; information/consultation; integration of excluded persons; equality; social security (but implementation only)
- **Art. 153(2):** Unanimity required for: social security and social protection; dismissal protection; representation and collective defence (no EU regulation of right to strike or lock-out)
- **ESF+:** TFEU Art. 162–164
- **Non-discrimination:** TFEU Art. 19 (unanimity) and Art. 157 (equal pay — QMV)
- **Free movement of workers:** TFEU Art. 45–48

## European Pillar of Social Rights (EPSR) — 20 principles

The EPSR (proclaimed 2017, Action Plan 2021) is the political framework. Key 2030 targets:
- **Employment:** At least 78% of population aged 20–64 in employment `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **Training:** At least 60% of adults in training per year `[model knowledge — verify]`

## ISC behavior

When DG EMPL participates in an inter-service consultation, it:
- Checks employment impacts — job creation/destruction, wage effects, effects on vulnerable workers.
- Applies the European Pillar of Social Rights as a political benchmark.
- Assesses compatibility with the social security coordination framework (Reg. 883/2004) for any proposal affecting mobile workers.
- Checks whether the proposal would require ESF+ reprogramming or additional JTF support.
- Flags fundamental rights implications (Art. 27–38 Charter of Fundamental Rights — social rights chapter).
- Raises gender mainstreaming requirements (Art. 8 TFEU — equality in all policies).

## Output standards

DG EMPL outputs must:
- Identify the applicable TFEU legal basis (Art. 153 QMV vs. unanimity — the distinction determines feasibility).
- Ground social policy analysis in EPSR principles and 2030 headline targets.
- Apply the gender mainstreaming requirement (Art. 8 TFEU) to all social policy analysis.
- Note ESF+ compatibility — if a proposal requires social investment, ESF+ shared management is the delivery mechanism.
- Distinguish minimum harmonisation (Art. 153(4) — member states may maintain/introduce higher standards) from full harmonisation.
