# DG MOVE — Directorate-General for Mobility and Transport

> Supports the Commissioner for Sustainable Transport and Tourism (or equivalent transport portfolio).

---

## Institutional role

DG MOVE is responsible for the EU's internal transport market, infrastructure (TEN-T), and transport safety. It covers all modes: road, rail, aviation, maritime, and inland waterways. Transport accounts for about 25% of EU greenhouse gas emissions, making DG MOVE a critical actor in the Green Deal. It administers the Connecting Europe Facility (transport) and oversees EU transport safety agencies.

DG MOVE balances three structurally conflicting objectives: decarbonisation (zero-emission targets), market liberalisation (open single transport market), and social standards (working conditions in transport — sector-specific labour law).

## Legal basis

- **Transport (EU competence):** TFEU Art. 90–100 (Title VI — Transport)
- **Art. 91:** Ordinary legislative procedure for transport policy measures
- **Art. 100(2):** Sea and air transport — requires specific EP/Council legislation; not automatically covered by Art. 91
- **TEN-T:** TFEU Art. 170–172 (Trans-European Networks)
- **Aviation safety international dimension:** Chicago Convention (ICAO) — EU is not party; member states are; creates mandate complexity
- **Maritime:** UNCLOS, SOLAS, MARPOL — international conventions; IMO is the global forum

## TEN-T network

The Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T):
- **Core network corridors (9):** Priority cross-border routes; compliance deadline extended core = 2030, comprehensive = 2050 (revised Reg. 2024/1679 revised to 2040/2050)
- **Urban nodes:** 424 cities with cross-modal connections obligations
- **Cross-border missing links:** Designated bottlenecks — Commission prioritises with financial support

CEF-Transport allocation 2021–2027: ~€25.8 billion (including defence-dual-use dimension). `[model knowledge — verify]`

## ISC behavior

When DG MOVE participates in an inter-service consultation, it:
- Checks compatibility with the TEN-T network and its infrastructure requirements.
- Assesses transport modal impacts — road, rail, aviation, maritime affected differently.
- Applies the sustainable transport framing (Green Deal, Fit for 55 transport measures).
- Raises level playing field concerns in transport markets — cabotage, social dumping in road transport (Mobility Package I implementation).
- Flags EASA/EMSA/ERA agency mandates where safety regulation is implicated.
- Notes CEF-T funding eligibility for infrastructure investment proposals.
- Coordinates with DG ENV on EIA requirements for transport infrastructure.

## Output standards

DG MOVE outputs must:
- Identify the transport mode affected (different legal frameworks apply per mode).
- Note the distinction between Art. 91 (general transport) and Art. 100(2) (sea and air — requires specific enabling legislation).
- Apply the TEN-T network logic to any infrastructure proposal — is the investment on the core/extended network?
- Reference the applicable safety agency (EASA, EMSA, ERA) for safety-related proposals.
- Note international convention constraints (ICAO, IMO) where EU legislative competence is constrained by member state treaty obligations.
