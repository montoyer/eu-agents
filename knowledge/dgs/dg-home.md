# DG HOME — Directorate-General for Migration and Home Affairs

> Supports the Commissioner for Home Affairs (or Executive Vice-President for Justice, Home Affairs, and Internal Security, depending on Commission configuration).

---

## Institutional role

DG HOME covers the EU's Area of Freedom, Security and Justice (AFSJ) — the treaty framework for internal security and migration. Its remit spans:
1. **Migration and asylum** — legal migration, asylum procedures, borders management
2. **Internal security** — counter-terrorism, organised crime, Schengen, police cooperation
3. **Border management** — Frontex oversight, Schengen governance

The AFSJ is politically one of the most sensitive areas of EU competence. Many measures require unanimous Council votes or operate through opt-out mechanisms (Denmark permanent opt-out; Ireland and UK selective opt-in). This structural constraint shapes what DG HOME can achieve legislatively.

## Legal basis

- **AFSJ — shared competence:** TFEU Title V (Art. 67–89)
- **Borders/asylum/migration:** TFEU Art. 77–79
- **Police cooperation:** TFEU Art. 87–89 — **unanimity** for operational police cooperation measures; QMV for administrative cooperation
- **Judicial cooperation (criminal):** TFEU Art. 82–86 — **emergency brake mechanism** — any member state can refer to European Council if core criminal justice principles are affected
- **Denmark opt-out:** Denmark has a permanent opt-out from Title V measures — does not participate in JHA legislation (except those building on Schengen acquis with Danish opt-in option)
- **Ireland/UK (pre-Brexit):** Selective opt-in — Ireland participates case by case

## New Pact on Migration and Asylum (2020–2024)

After years of deadlock post-2015 migration crisis, the Pact legislative package was adopted in 2024:
- **Mandatory solidarity:** Member states must choose between relocation, financial contributions, or operational support
- **Accelerated border procedures:** At border for applicants from countries with low recognition rates (<20%)
- **EURODAC:** Expanded to include all third-country nationals (not just asylum seekers)
- **Crisis regulation:** Fast-track procedures and solidarity in mass influx situations

Implementation deadline: 2026. DG HOME coordinates the national action plans.

## ISC behavior

When DG HOME participates in an inter-service consultation, it:
- Checks fundamental rights compatibility (Art. 18 Charter: right to asylum; Art. 19: non-refoulement; Art. 6: liberty and security; Art. 7: private life).
- Applies the Schengen governance framework — internal border controls must be temporary, last resort, proportionate.
- Raises counter-terrorism and serious crime risk dimensions for proposals involving large data processing (digital ID, biometrics, financial data).
- Checks Europol consultation requirements for law enforcement-related proposals.
- Notes opt-out implications for Denmark and potential opt-in cases for Ireland.
- Coordinates with DG JUST on fundamental rights and data protection dimensions.

## Output standards

DG HOME outputs must:
- Identify the correct legal basis and the applicable voting rule (QMV vs. unanimity — critical for police cooperation and certain judicial measures).
- Note the opt-out/opt-in status of Denmark and Ireland for any AFSJ measure.
- Apply the non-refoulement principle (Art. 19 CFR + Geneva Convention) to any returns or border measure.
- Cite the Schengen Borders Code for any measure affecting free movement within the Schengen area.
- Reference Europol consultation requirements where measures affect EU law enforcement cooperation.
