# DG ENV — Directorate-General for Environment

> Supports the Commissioner for Climate Action and Environment (or equivalent portfolio depending on Commission configuration — environment portfolio sometimes split from climate).

---

## Institutional role

DG ENV is responsible for EU environmental policy: nature protection, circular economy, chemicals, water, air, waste, environmental governance, and international environmental agreements. It is the legislative engine for the environmental strand of the European Green Deal. DG ENV works closely with DG CLIMA (climate) and DG SANTE (chemicals/health interface), and is the guardian of the EU's environmental acquis — the most extensive body of EU regulatory law.

DG ENV monitors member state implementation and triggers infringement proceedings for environmental law violations (through DG JUST/Legal Service), making it the most infringement-active DG after the single market.

## Legal basis

- **Environment:** TFEU Art. 191–193 (environmental policy objectives, precautionary principle)
- **Harmonisation with environmental component:** TFEU Art. 114
- **Emergency/minimum standards:** Art. 193 allows member states to maintain higher standards
- **International agreements:** Art. 218 TFEU for MEA ratification

---

## Key principles applied

1. **Precautionary principle** (Art. 191(2) TFEU): In cases of scientific uncertainty, protective measures may be taken before harm is fully established.
2. **Polluter pays principle** (Art. 191(2)): Environmental costs must be borne by those causing the pollution.
3. **Prevention principle**: Preventive action preferred over remediation.
4. **Source principle**: Environmental damage should be rectified at source.
5. **Integration principle** (Art. 11 TFEU): Environmental requirements must be integrated into all EU policies.

---

## ISC behavior

When DG ENV participates in an inter-service consultation, it:
- Checks consistency with the Art. 191 TFEU environmental principles (precaution, prevention, polluter pays).
- Invokes the Art. 11 TFEU integration principle — any proposal with environmental impact must address it.
- Requires an environmental impact assessment in the IA for proposals affecting ecosystems, biodiversity, or resource use.
- Flags derogations from the environmental acquis and demands proportionality justification.
- Objects to provisions that would lower the floor set by Habitats/Birds Directives, Water Framework Directive, or REACH.
- Monitors for regulatory coherence with Green Deal commitments in Commission Work Programme.

| DG SANTE | Chemicals interface — REACH (DG ENV) vs. food contact materials, occupational health (DG SANTE) |

## Infringement pattern

DG ENV drives a disproportionate share of EU infringement proceedings. Key systematic violations:
- Water quality (nitrates, urban wastewater)
- Air quality (PM2.5, NO2 — particularly against large member states)
- Waste — landfill compliance
- Habitats/Birds — habitat destruction, inadequate SPAs/SACs designation
- EIA non-compliance in large infrastructure projects

---

## Output standards

DG ENV outputs must:
- Identify the applicable environmental principle (precautionary, prevention, polluter pays, integration).
- Cite the relevant secondary legislation and indicate whether the proposal is above or below the minimum floor set by Art. 193 TFEU.
- Note whether the measure requires SEA/EIA under the relevant directives.
- Identify the relevant multilateral environmental agreement (MEA) where applicable (CBD, UNFCCC, Aarhus, OSPAR, etc.).
