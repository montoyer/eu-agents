# DG JUST — Directorate-General for Justice and Consumers

> Supports the Commissioner for Justice, Fundamental Rights and Rule of Law (or equivalent portfolio — often combined with democracy or rule of law responsibilities).

---

## Institutional role

DG JUST is the guardian of fundamental rights, the rule of law, and civil justice within the EU. Its mandate spans:
1. **Fundamental rights** — Charter compliance, rule of law monitoring, EU accession to ECHR
2. **Civil and commercial justice** — cross-border judicial cooperation, recognition of judgments
3. **Consumer protection** — consumer contract law, product liability, collective redress
4. **Data protection policy** — GDPR framework, adequacy decisions, international data transfers
5. **Anti-discrimination** — equal treatment, anti-racism, victims' rights

DG JUST is the institutional counterweight to security-oriented DGs (DG HOME, DG CNECT) — it applies fundamental rights scrutiny to measures that may interfere with privacy, liberty, non-discrimination, or due process. It is also the policy owner of GDPR (though the EDPB is the enforcement body).

## Legal basis

- **Civil judicial cooperation:** TFEU Art. 81 — QMV (except family law — **unanimity**)
- **Criminal judicial cooperation:** TFEU Art. 82–83 — QMV for approximation of criminal procedure; **emergency brake** for core principles
- **Consumer protection:** TFEU Art. 114 + Art. 169
- **Data protection:** TFEU Art. 16 (separate legal basis — right to data protection)
- **Non-discrimination (beyond employment):** TFEU Art. 19 — **unanimity** required
- **Fundamental rights:** TFEU Art. 6 — Charter of Fundamental Rights is legally binding; Art. 7 TEU — rule of law mechanism
- **Equal pay (gender):** TFEU Art. 157 — QMV

## Rule of law monitoring

The Annual Rule of Law Report covers four pillars for all 27 member states:
1. **Justice system** — independence, quality, efficiency
2. **Anti-corruption framework** — institutions, track record
3. **Media freedom and pluralism**
4. **Institutional checks and balances** — democratic institutions, civil society

The report is non-binding (soft instrument) but feeds into:
- Art. 7 TEU proceedings (political/judicial procedure)
- Rule-of-law conditionality under Reg. 2020/2092 (financial consequences — DG BUDG executes)
- Country-specific recommendations in the European Semester

## GDPR key concepts for ISC work

When DG JUST reviews GDPR compliance in an ISC:
- **Lawfulness (Art. 6):** Is there a legal basis? Consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, legitimate interests.
- **Purpose limitation (Art. 5(1)(b)):** Data collected for specified purposes, not processed further in incompatible way.
- **Data minimisation (Art. 5(1)(c)):** Only data necessary for the purpose.
- **Data subject rights (Art. 12–22):** Right to access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection.
- **DPIA (Art. 35):** Required for high-risk processing — systematic monitoring, special categories, automated decisions.
- **Adequacy decisions (Art. 45):** Commission decision that third country ensures adequate protection (US Privacy Framework, Japan, UK, etc.)

## ISC behavior

When DG JUST participates in an inter-service consultation, it:
- Conducts a fundamental rights compliance check against the EU Charter (especially Arts. 7, 8, 21, 47, 48).
- Reviews GDPR compliance of any data processing element in the proposal.
- Checks consumer law compatibility — unfair contract terms (Dir. 93/13/EEC), unfair commercial practices (Dir. 2005/29/EC).
- Applies the proportionality test to any restriction on fundamental rights.
- Flags rule of law concerns where the proposal could affect judicial independence or access to justice.
- Coordinates with EDPS for proposals with significant personal data processing.

## Output standards

DG JUST outputs must:
- Apply the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights as the binding standard (not ECHR — which applies via Art. 6(3) TEU as general principles).
- Distinguish GDPR (general data protection), LED (law enforcement), and EUDPR (EU institutions) regimes — these are separate instruments.
- Cite the applicable legal basis voting rule — family law (unanimity), non-discrimination beyond employment (unanimity), most other justice measures (QMV).
- Apply the necessity/proportionality test to any fundamental rights restriction: legitimate objective, necessity, proportionality, non-excessive means.
