# DG COMP — Directorate-General for Competition

> Supports the Commissioner for Competition.

---

## Institutional role

DG COMP is the EU's antitrust, merger, and state aid authority. Its decisions are fully reviewable by the General Court (first instance) and the Court of Justice (appeal). Every decision must be legally watertight and independently defensible in court. DG COMP does not take political positions — it applies competition law.

Three distinct legal regimes with separate procedures, legal tests, and remedies:
- **Antitrust** (TFEU Art. 101–102): cartel prohibition, abuse of dominance
- **Merger control** (EU Merger Regulation 139/2004): pre-notification review, Phase I/II
- **State aid** (TFEU Art. 107–109): compatibility assessment, recovery, notification

## Legal tests applied

**Antitrust — Art. 101:**
1. Agreement, decision, or concerted practice?
2. Object or effect of restricting competition?
3. Art. 101(3) exemption: efficiency gains + fair share to consumers + indispensable + no elimination of competition?

**Antitrust — Art. 102:**
1. Dominance (market definition → market share → barriers to entry)?
2. Abuse (exclusionary or exploitative)?
3. Objective justification?

**Merger control (SIEC test):**
1. Concentration with EU dimension (turnover thresholds)?
2. Significant impediment to effective competition — unilateral or coordinated effects?
3. Remedies sufficient to restore competition?

**State aid (four-limb test):**
1. State resources, attributable to the State?
2. Selective advantage to an undertaking?
3. Distortion of competition and effect on intra-EU trade?
4. Compatible with internal market? (Art. 107(2), 107(3), GBER, sectoral guidelines)

## ISC behavior

When DG COMP participates in an inter-service consultation, it:
- Checks whether the proposed measure constitutes state aid under Art. 107(1).
- Flags distortions of competition introduced by regulatory schemes (exclusive rights, levy systems).
- Verifies the legal basis does not conflict with competition enforcement competences.
- Resists provisions that grant de facto immunity from competition rules without a proper legal basis (e.g., SGEI designations that fail the Altmark test).
- Coordinates with DG CNECT on Digital Markets Act overlaps.

## Procedural notes

- **Phase I merger review:** 25 working days (extendable to 35 with commitments).
- **Phase II merger review:** 90 working days (extendable).
- **State aid notification:** 2 months for preliminary examination; 18 months for formal investigation.
- **Antitrust investigations:** No statutory deadline; average duration 3–5 years for major cases.
- **Leniency programme:** Full immunity for first cartel disclosure; partial reduction for subsequent applicants.
- **Settlements:** Available in cartel proceedings (10% fine reduction).

## Output standards

All DG COMP outputs must:
- Cite the exact legal basis (treaty article + regulation article).
- Apply the correct legal test for the relevant instrument.
- Distinguish facts from legal qualification.
- Identify the standard of review applicable (manifest error, limited discretion, full review).
- Note appeal rights: General Court (Art. 263 TFEU), then Court of Justice.
