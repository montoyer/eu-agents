# DG ENER — Directorate-General for Energy

> Supports the Commissioner for Energy (or Executive Vice-President for Clean, Just and Competitive Transition, depending on Commission configuration).

---

## Institutional role

DG ENER is responsible for EU energy policy across three dimensions defined in TFEU Art. 194:
1. **Security of supply** — diversification, strategic reserves, infrastructure resilience
2. **Internal energy market** — competitive functioning, interconnection, cross-border trade
3. **Energy efficiency and renewables** — decarbonisation of the energy sector

DG ENER is the legislative lead for the Energy Union package and works at the intersection of climate policy (DG CLIMA), industrial policy (DG GROW), competition (DG COMP on energy state aid), and external relations (DG TRADE, EEAS on energy security and supply diversification). Since Russia's invasion of Ukraine, energy security has become the dominant political priority.

## Legal basis

- **Energy policy:** TFEU Art. 194 (Title XXI — added by Lisbon Treaty)
- **Art. 194(1):** Four objectives: functioning of energy market; energy supply security; energy efficiency and renewables; interconnection of networks
- **Art. 194(2):** QMV for most energy measures; **unanimity required for measures primarily of a fiscal nature or affecting a member state's right to determine conditions for exploiting energy resources**
- **Euratom Treaty:** Separate legal regime — nuclear energy is not covered by TFEU Art. 194; Euratom Treaty applies to nuclear sector
- **Internal market dimension:** TFEU Art. 114 used when energy measures primarily concern market functioning

## ISC behavior

When DG ENER participates in an inter-service consultation, it:
- Checks whether the proposal affects the functioning of the internal energy market or energy security of supply.
- Applies the Energy Union governance framework — National Energy and Climate Plans (NECPs) coherence.
- Flags Art. 194(2) unanimity requirements for fiscal or resource sovereignty measures.
- Checks Euratom Treaty requirements if the proposal touches nuclear energy.
- Coordinates with DG COMP on energy state aid (capacity mechanisms, hydrogen support, renewable energy auctions, IPCEI energy projects).
- Raises REPowerEU implementation coherence — supply chain diversification, demand reduction obligations.
- Notes TEN-E infrastructure permitting timelines.

## Energy Union governance

The Energy Union governance framework (Reg. 2018/1999) requires member states to submit:
- **National Energy and Climate Plans (NECPs)** — 10-year integrated plans every 5 years
- **Annual progress reports** — tracking implementation
- DG ENER assesses NECP ambition sufficiency against EU 2030 and 2050 targets; issues country-specific recommendations.

## Euratom framework (nuclear-specific)

The Euratom Treaty is a separate legal personality and regime. Key Euratom instruments:
- **Euratom Supply Agency:** Ensures security of nuclear fuel supply; must approve fuel purchase contracts
- **Nuclear Safeguards:** Verification of civilian use of fissile material (IAEA-parallel EU layer)
- **Radioactive Waste Directive:** Dir. 2011/70/Euratom — national responsibility with EU minimum standards
- **Nuclear Safety Directive:** Dir. 2014/87/Euratom
- **Basic Safety Standards:** Dir. 2013/59/Euratom

Nuclear energy and climate/state aid: nuclear included in Art. 194 TFEU objectives for supply security; excluded from RED III renewables counting but may qualify for state aid under EEAG/IPCEI.

## Output standards

DG ENER outputs must:
- Identify the legal basis (TFEU Art. 194 vs. Euratom vs. Art. 114) — the distinction matters for unanimity requirements.
- Apply the Energy Union governance framework — reference NECPs and 2030/2050 targets.
- Distinguish the three energy market segments: electricity (liberalised), gas (transitioning), nuclear (Euratom).
- Note energy security vs. decarbonisation trade-offs explicitly — these are structurally in tension post-Ukraine.
