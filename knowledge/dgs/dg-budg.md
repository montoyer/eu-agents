# DG BUDG — Directorate-General for Budget

> Supports the Commissioner for Budget and Anti-Fraud.

---

## Institutional role

DG BUDG is the Commission's financial management authority. It prepares and executes the EU budget, manages the Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF), develops the EU own resources system, and sets the financial regulation framework for all EU expenditure. DG BUDG is the internal financial watchdog: it ensures that every expenditure of EU funds is legal, regular, and in accordance with sound financial management principles.

DG BUDG does not manage spending programmes (those are managed by sectoral DGs) but sets the rules under which they operate. It is the lead DG for the Financial Regulation (which all DGs must comply with) and the discharge procedure with the European Parliament.

## Legal basis

- **EU finances:** TFEU Art. 310–325 (Title II of Part Six — financial provisions)
- **Art. 310:** Budget must be balanced; all revenue and expenditure must be included (unity and universality)
- **Art. 314–315:** Annual budget procedure (Parliament + Council codecision)
- **Art. 317:** Commission implements the budget on its own responsibility — principle of sound financial management
- **Art. 319:** Discharge procedure — Parliament grants discharge following Court of Auditors report
- **Art. 325:** Protection of the EU's financial interests (anti-fraud)
- **Own resources:** TFEU Art. 311; requires Council unanimity + national ratification

---

## Budget principles (Financial Regulation, Art. 6–13)

1. **Unity and universality** — all revenue and expenditure in a single budget document
2. **Annuality** — appropriations authorised for one financial year (carryovers strictly regulated)
3. **Equilibrium** — no deficit; EU cannot borrow to finance operating expenditure (NGEU is an exception under Art. 122 TFEU emergency instrument)
4. **Unit of account** — euro
5. **Specification** — appropriations tied to specific purposes
6. **Sound financial management** — economy, efficiency, effectiveness (the 3Es)
7. **Transparency** — publication of budget and beneficiary data (Financial Transparency System)

## Annual budget procedure

```
May–June:         Commission preliminary draft budget (PDB)
July–August:      Council first reading (general approach)
September:        Parliament committee position
October:          Conciliation period (21 days, Parliament + Council)
November:         Joint text agreed → adoption
December:         Budget in force (if not agreed → 1/12 provisional appropriations rule, Art. 315 TFEU)
```

## Own resources system

EU revenue comes from three sources:
1. **Traditional own resources (TOR):** Customs duties (~15%) — collected by member states, 75% remitted to EU
2. **VAT-based own resource:** Uniform 0.3% call rate on harmonised VAT base (~10%)
3. **GNI-based resource:** Uniform call rate on GNI (~70%) — balancing resource; determines member state contributions

**New own resources under development:**
- CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism) revenues — 75% to EU budget from 2026 `[model knowledge — verify]`
- ETS revenue share — 25% of auctioning revenues
- BEFIT contribution (Business in Europe: Framework for Income Taxation) — pending `[model knowledge — verify]`
- Statistical digital levy — under discussion

## ISC behavior

When DG BUDG participates in an inter-service consultation, it:
- Checks Financial Regulation compliance — is the proposed spending instrument compatible with title management, authorisation chains, and audit requirements?
- Flags budgetary implications — does the proposal require a financial statement (Art. 83 FR)? Does it stay within MFF ceilings?
- Checks rule-of-law conditionality exposure — does the proposal involve funding to member states with pending conditionality cases?
- Reviews additionality requirements for ESIF and shared management instruments.
- Flags off-budget instrument risks (EU guarantees, EFSD+, blending) — contingent liabilities must be provisioned.
- Coordinates with OLAF on anti-fraud provisions in new spending programmes.

## Relationship with Court of Auditors

The European Court of Auditors (ECA) provides the Statement of Assurance (DAS) on the legality and regularity of EU accounts. DG BUDG coordinates the Commission's response to ECA annual reports, special reports, and opinions. A persistent ECA qualification triggers Discharge scrutiny by the European Parliament Committee on Budgetary Control (CONT).

---

## Output standards

DG BUDG outputs must:
- Apply the seven budget principles (unity, universality, annuality, equilibrium, unit of account, specification, sound financial management).
- Identify the MFF heading and sub-ceiling for any proposed expenditure.
- Reference the Financial Regulation articles governing the proposed management mode (direct, shared, indirect).
- Distinguish commitment appropriations from payment appropriations.
- Flag contingent liabilities (EU guarantees, financial instruments) and their provisioning requirements.
