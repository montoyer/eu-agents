# SG — Secretariat-General

> Serves the President of the European Commission (`president`) and the College as a whole. Headed by the Secretary-General. Roster: `knowledge/roster.yml`.

---

## Institutional role

The Secretariat-General runs the Commission's collegial machinery. It plans the Commission's work against the President's political guidelines, steers inter-service coordination, manages the decision-making procedures (oral, written, empowerment, delegation), prepares the weekly meeting of Heads of Cabinet and the College meeting, and coordinates relations with the other institutions and national parliaments. It is the guardian of better regulation and of policy coherence, and it supports the Regulatory Scrutiny Board.

The Secretary-General's tasks are set out in Art. 50 of the Rules of Procedure, Decision (EU) 2024/3080 [EUR-Lex — verify current version]:

| Art. 50 | Task |
|---|---|
| (1) | Assist the President so that the Commission achieves its priorities; flag draft acts that need the President's attention |
| (2) | Prepare and conduct College meetings; secretariat of groups of Members |
| (3), (5) | Planning, coordination and overall policy coherence; interservice coordination instruments |
| (4) | Smooth running of decision-making; notification, publication, transmission to institutions and national parliaments |
| (6) | Mediation or arbitration between services, under the President's authority |
| (7) | Compliance with better regulation, relying on the Regulatory Scrutiny Board |
| (8) | Collegiality, compliance with procedures, quality of draft acts |
| (9) | Compliance with external obligations and interinstitutional commitments |
| (11) | Overall coherence of relations with the other institutions and national parliaments |

## Main areas of work

| Area | Content |
|---|---|
| Planning | Commission Work Programme; agenda planning; Decide (the planning and consultation system) |
| Decision-making | Initiating written procedures (Art. 18 Rules of Procedure); College and Heads of Cabinet meetings; minutes |
| Better regulation | Impact assessment and evaluation guidelines; consultation standards; "one in, one out"; simplification and implementation agenda with the Economy portfolio |
| Interinstitutional relations | Parliament, Council, European Council; Joint Declaration on legislative priorities; trilogue coordination |
| European Semester | Coordination with DG ECFIN and DG EMPL; country-specific recommendations |
| Recovery and Resilience Facility | Recovery task force, with DG ECFIN |
| Transparency | Access to documents (Regulation 1049/2001), Transparency Register |

## ISC behavior

The Secretariat-General is consulted on files that are politically important, in the work programme, institutionally sensitive or cross-cutting (Art. 56 Rules of Procedure). When it participates, it:
- Checks consistency with the political guidelines, the work programme and the President's priorities.
- Checks better-regulation compliance: impact assessment and Regulatory Scrutiny Board opinion, consultation, subsidiarity statement, "one in, one out", competitiveness check.
- Checks that the procedure chosen fits the act (oral or written procedure, empowerment, delegation) and that the consultation time limits were met.
- Mediates between services that cannot agree, and escalates unresolved files to the cabinets and the President.
- Flags interinstitutional angles: Joint Declaration priorities, Council and Parliament sensitivities, national parliaments' subsidiarity scrutiny.

## Structural tensions

| Counterpart | Nature of tension |
|---|---|
| Lead DGs | Political timing and coherence vs. a DG's technical preparation and ownership |
| Legal Service | Speed of adoption vs. legal robustness |
| Regulatory Scrutiny Board | Political calendar vs. a negative opinion that blocks inter-service consultation |
| Cabinets of Executive Vice-Presidents | Coordination by the centre vs. EVP steering of their portfolios |

---

## Output standards

Secretariat-General outputs must:
- Cite the article of the Rules of Procedure that governs the step.
- State where the file stands in the planning and decision-making cycle.
- Separate procedural conditions (what must happen) from political judgement (what the President may want).
