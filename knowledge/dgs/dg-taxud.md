# DG TAXUD — Directorate-General for Taxation and Customs Union

> Supports the Commissioner for Economy and Taxation (or equivalent portfolio — tax and customs sometimes split from economy/finance Commissioner).

---

## Institutional role

DG TAXUD covers two distinct competences with very different political dynamics:

1. **Customs Union** — the EU's exclusive competence; harmonised tariff, customs procedures, product classification, origin rules. All member states apply identical external tariffs. Customs policy is trade policy in practice — politically linked to DG TRADE.

2. **Taxation** — one of the most sovereignty-sensitive areas; **unanimity in Council** required for all direct and indirect tax harmonisation. This structural constraint is the defining feature of DG TAXUD's legislative strategy: most tax initiatives either rely on unanimity negotiations or find workarounds through enhanced cooperation.

DG TAXUD designs and proposes tax legislation but does not enforce it — enforcement is a member state competence. The Commission can initiate infringement proceedings for non-compliance (notably VAT directive transposition) but cannot audit individual taxpayers.

## Legal basis

- **Customs Union:** TFEU Art. 30–37 (exclusive competence, Art. 3(1)(a) TFEU); **QMV**
- **Indirect taxation (VAT, excise):** TFEU Art. 113 — **unanimity** required
- **Direct taxation:** TFEU Art. 115 — **unanimity** required (approximation of laws affecting internal market functioning)
- **Enhanced cooperation:** TFEU Art. 20 + Art. 329 — minimum 9 MS can proceed without unanimity (used for FTT, EU patent, matrimonial property)
- No specific tax base Treaty article — DG TAXUD relies on Art. 113 (indirect) or Art. 114 (internal market) as alternative basis where possible

---

## The unanimity constraint

Every significant DG TAXUD legislative proposal must navigate the unanimity requirement in Council. In practice:
- **Direct tax proposals have a low success rate** — Hungary, Ireland, Luxembourg, Malta, Estonia often form a blocking minority on corporate tax proposals
- **Enhanced cooperation** has been used (FTT — 10 MS) or threatened as a way to pressure holdouts
- **OECD-level agreements** (BEPS, Pillar Two) have provided cover for EU legislative follow-up — the Pillar Two Directive (15% global minimum tax) was adopted unanimously in December 2022 only because the OECD framework was a fait accompli
- **Infringement for TFEU Art. 18 non-discrimination** (as opposed to tax harmonisation) can be used without unanimity

## CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism)

CBAM (Reg. 2023/956) is a joint DG TAXUD/DG CLIMA instrument:
- Importers of CBAM goods (steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, electricity, hydrogen) must purchase CBAM certificates
- CBAM certificate price = EU ETS carbon price
- Prevents carbon leakage — removes competitive disadvantage for EU producers subject to ETS
- WTO compatibility: contested — Paris Agreement Art. 4 and GATT Art. XX (environment exception) are the defences
- DG TAXUD manages the CBAM registry and authorised declarant system; DG CLIMA manages alignment with ETS

## ISC behavior

When DG TAXUD participates in an inter-service consultation, it:
- Flags tax provisions that require unanimity in Council — assesses political feasibility and alternative legal bases.
- Checks customs classification implications for any proposal involving traded goods.
- Applies the CBAM framework — any proposal creating carbon price disparity may interact with CBAM.
- Reviews VAT treatment of new business models, digital services, public sector activities.
- Coordinates with DG COMP on harmful tax competition (Code of Conduct Group) and state aid via tax rulings.
- Checks DAC (Directive on Administrative Cooperation) reporting obligations if the proposal involves new cross-border financial flows.

## Structural tensions

| Counterpart | Nature of tension |
|---|---|
| DG COMP | Tax rulings as state aid — DG TAXUD defends tax policy autonomy; DG COMP applies state aid rules to tax measures |
| DG TRADE | Customs rules affect market access — TAXUD sets the tariff; TRADE negotiates the exceptions |
| DG ENER | Energy Tax Directive reform — carbon pricing through energy taxation vs. ETS (double regulation risk) |
| DG GROW | Corporate tax reform (BEFIT) vs. business environment competitiveness concerns |
| DG BUDG | CBAM revenues to EU budget (own resource) — TAXUD administers, BUDG benefits |
| DG HOME | Anti-money laundering — AMLA interacts with tax evasion, DAC reporting |

## Output standards

DG TAXUD outputs must:
- Identify the applicable legal basis (Art. 113 indirect tax — unanimity; Art. 115 direct tax — unanimity; Art. 30–37 customs — QMV).
- Note the political feasibility constraint from unanimity — flag which member states are likely blocking minorities.
- Apply the customs classification (CN code, HS code, combined nomenclature) where traded goods are involved.
- Distinguish tax harmonisation (approximation of laws) from tax coordination (soft convergence) — different instruments, different Treaty bases.
- Cross-reference CBAM and ETS when carbon pricing dimensions are involved.
