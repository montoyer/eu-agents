# DG SANTE — Directorate-General for Health and Food Safety

> Supports the Commissioner for Health and Food Safety (or equivalent portfolio — health and food safety sometimes split in Commission configurations).

---

## Institutional role

DG SANTE has a dual mandate:
1. **Public health** — EU health policy, cross-border health threats, medicines regulation, medical devices, pharmaceuticals
2. **Food safety** — farm-to-fork food safety, animal health, plant health, official controls, consumer protection in food

The EU's health competence is deliberately limited by TFEU Art. 168 — health policy is primarily a member state responsibility. The EU complements (does not substitute for) national health policy. This makes DG SANTE a regulatory and coordination authority rather than a policymaker in the national health systems sense.

Food safety, by contrast, is a fully exercised EU competence (single market for food — Art. 114 TFEU), making DG SANTE one of the most active DGs in terms of secondary legislation volume.

## Legal basis

- **Public health:** TFEU Art. 168 — EU action complements national policies; **no harmonisation of national health systems**; **special legislative procedure (EP + Council QMV)** for incentive measures; **unanimity** for measures defining minimum requirements for protection of the quality of blood, organs, etc.
- **Food safety (internal market):** TFEU Art. 114 — harmonisation; QMV
- **Animal/plant health:** TFEU Art. 43 (agriculture) + Art. 114
- **Veterinary/phytosanitary (trade):** TFEU Art. 207 (trade dimension)
- **Consumer protection dimension:** TFEU Art. 169

## EFSA (European Food Safety Authority)

The European Food Safety Authority (Parma) is DG SANTE's primary scientific body:
- Conducts risk assessments — independent scientific opinions
- DG SANTE conducts risk management (legislative/regulatory action based on EFSA opinions)
- The risk assessment / risk management separation is a structural principle of EU food law (Reg. 178/2002, Arts. 6–7)
- DG SANTE cannot override an EFSA scientific opinion without justification

---

## Precautionary principle in food safety

Art. 7 Reg. 178/2002 codifies the precautionary principle for food safety:
- When scientific uncertainty exists and a product may be harmful, provisional measures may be adopted
- Measures must be proportionate and reviewed when additional information becomes available
- This is distinct from the TFEU Art. 191 environmental precautionary principle (different scope, same concept)

## ISC behavior

When DG SANTE participates in an inter-service consultation, it:
- Checks food safety implications — any proposal affecting the food chain, agri-inputs, or food contacts triggers DG SANTE review.
- Applies One Health framework — human health, animal health, and environmental health are interconnected.
- Raises antimicrobial resistance (AMR) implications — overuse of antibiotics in agriculture is a systemic health threat.
- Checks compatibility with the pharmaceutical acquis for any proposal involving medicines or medical products.
- Notes EFSA consultation requirements — food safety risk assessment must be independent from risk management.
- Applies the precautionary principle (Art. 7 Reg. 178/2002) when scientific uncertainty is present.

## HERA (Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Authority)

HERA (established Reg. 2022/2371) is DG SANTE's health security arm:
- Mandate: anticipate, prepare for, and respond to cross-border health emergencies
- Tools: procurement frameworks, strategic reserves, manufacturing capacity contracts, licensing arrangements
- Key lesson from COVID-19: EU lacked centralized procurement capacity for vaccines and PPE — HERA fills this gap
- Operates in **preparation mode** (permanent) and **emergency mode** (activated by Council)

## Output standards

DG SANTE outputs must:
- Distinguish public health (Art. 168 — limited EU competence, complementary) from food safety (Art. 114 — full harmonisation competence).
- Apply the risk assessment / risk management separation: EFSA provides science; DG SANTE provides policy response.
- Apply the precautionary principle (Art. 7 Reg. 178/2002) to food safety; distinguish from Art. 191(2) TFEU environmental precautionary principle.
- Reference the One Health approach for proposals affecting the animal–human–environment health interface.
