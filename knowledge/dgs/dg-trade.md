# DG TRADE — Directorate-General for Trade

> Supports the Commissioner for Trade and Economic Security (or equivalent trade portfolio).

---

## Institutional role

DG TRADE manages the EU's Common Commercial Policy (CCP) — an exclusive EU competence under TFEU Art. 207. The EU speaks with one voice in trade: the Commission negotiates all trade agreements, the Council authorises negotiations and adopts negotiating directives, and the European Parliament gives consent to concluded agreements.

DG TRADE sits at the intersection of economics (market access, EU export interests) and geopolitics (trade as a tool of EU foreign policy, economic security, sanctions). Since 2021 it has expanded significantly into trade defence, critical raw materials, and economic coercion — reflecting the shift to "open strategic autonomy."

## Legal basis

- **Common Commercial Policy:** TFEU Art. 207 — **exclusive competence** (Art. 3(1)(e) TFEU)
- **Art. 207(1):** Uniform principles — tariff changes, trade agreements, trade liberalisation, export policy, trade defence
- **Art. 207(4):** Mixed agreements for cultural/audiovisual, social/education (require unanimity and may require national ratification)
- **Art. 218 TFEU:** Procedure for negotiating and concluding international agreements — Council authorises and concludes; EP consents
- **Post-Opinion 2/15 (CETA):** FTA scope — trade provisions are EU exclusive; investment (beyond portfolio) requires mixed agreement + national ratification

## Trade agreement structure

Modern EU FTAs ("new generation") include:
1. **Market access:** tariffs, quotas, rules of origin, customs
2. **Trade in services and investment** (to extent of EU-only competence)
3. **Technical barriers to trade (TBT):** mutual recognition, harmonisation
4. **Sanitary and phytosanitary measures (SPS):** food safety recognition
5. **Intellectual property**
6. **Government procurement**
7. **Trade and sustainable development (TSD):** ILO conventions, Paris Agreement, biodiversity
8. **Competition**
9. **Dispute settlement**

**TSD chapters** post-Lisbon are binding but enforcement has been contested — CJEU Opinion 2/15 confirmed EU competence; enforcement cases initiated (Korea). New approach: TSD chapters subject to standard trade dispute resolution (not just state-to-state consultations).

## Trade defence instruments

**Anti-dumping (AD):** Applied when imports are sold in the EU at below normal value AND cause material injury to EU industry.
- Normal value = domestic price in exporting country (or constructed value)
- Dumping margin = normal value minus export price
- Injury test: material injury to EU industry, threat of injury, or material retardation
- Procedure: 12–15 months; provisional measures at 9 months

**Anti-subsidy / countervailing duties (CVD):** Applied when imports are subsidised by a foreign government AND cause injury.
- Must identify specific, quantifiable subsidies
- Parallel but separate from AD

**Safeguards:** Applied when imports increase suddenly causing serious injury — no need to identify dumping or subsidy; WTO Art. XIX.

## ISC behavior

When DG TRADE participates in an inter-service consultation, it:
- Checks WTO compatibility — any measure affecting trade in goods or services must be assessed for GATT/GATS compliance (national treatment, MFN, quantitative restrictions).
- Flags market access implications for EU exporters in third countries.
- Assesses impact on ongoing FTA negotiations — if a proposal creates barriers to trade covered by a negotiation, it affects the EU's negotiating position.
- Checks IPI (International Procurement Instrument) relevance — reciprocity conditions for public procurement.
- Raises ACI (Anti-Coercion Instrument) relevance where a third country's economic pressure is involved.
- Coordinates with DG COMP on FSR interface (DG TRADE focuses on market access; DG COMP on domestic subsidy effects).

## Economic security agenda

Post-2022, DG TRADE expanded into:
- **De-risking from China** — identifying critical dependencies in supply chains (strategic goods, semiconductors, critical raw materials)
- **Export controls** — dual-use goods (Reg. 2021/821); alignment with G7 Export Controls Coordination
- **Critical Raw Materials partnerships** — Strategic Partnerships with Canada, Australia, Chile, etc.
- **Anti-Coercion Instrument (ACI)** — countermeasures against economic coercion by third countries

## Output standards

DG TRADE outputs must:
- Apply the exclusive competence framing (TFEU Art. 207) — the EU, not member states, speaks in trade.
- Distinguish EU-exclusive from mixed agreement scope (post-Opinion 2/15 Singapore).
- Apply the WTO consistency framework (GATT, GATS, TBT, SPS, TRIPS) to any trade-restrictive measure.
- Apply the three-limb trade defence test (dumping/subsidy + injury + Union interest) for TDI proposals.
- Note the difference between market access (removing barriers) and trade defence (adding barriers) — different legal logic and WTO rules.
